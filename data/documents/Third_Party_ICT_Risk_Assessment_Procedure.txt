1. Introduction

Corporate Banking Group (CBG) relies on third-party providers for software, cloud services,
infrastructure hosting, development support, and specialized technology. Third-party
relationships introduce risks including data leakage, service disruption, concentration
risk, and reduced control over the technology environment.

This procedure defines minimum requirements for assessing, onboarding, and monitoring third-
party ICT providers, ensuring risks are identified and managed in line with CBG risk
appetite and operational resilience objectives.

2. Scope

This procedure applies to ICT-related third parties, including:

- SaaS/IaaS/PaaS cloud providers and managed service providers.

- Vendors with remote access to CBG systems.

- Software suppliers (including open source support vendors) where CBG depends on vendor patches/support.

- Outsourced development, operations, or security services.

The procedure applies to new engagements, renewals, and material changes to existing
contracts.

3. Roles and Responsibilities

- Business Owner: sponsors the engagement, defines service requirements, and owns business impact decisions.

- ICT Service Owner: validates technical integration, architecture, and operational controls.

- TPRM (ICT): coordinates due diligence, risk assessments, and ongoing monitoring; maintains third-party inventory.

- Information Security: performs security assessment and defines security requirements; reviews high-risk findings.

- Procurement and Legal: ensures contracts include required clauses; manages negotiation and supplier obligations.

- Continuity/Resilience Team: assesses resilience, exit planning, and recovery capabilities for critical services.

4. Third-Party Classification and Criticality

Third parties must be classified based on:

- Data sensitivity: whether Confidential or Restricted data is processed.

- Service criticality: whether the service supports critical banking functions (payments, authentication, customer channels).

- Connectivity: network integration depth and remote access requirements.

- Replaceability and concentration: ease of switching, market availability, dependency level.

Critical third parties require enhanced due diligence, resilience assessment, and executive
oversight.

5. Due Diligence and Pre-Contract Assessment

Minimum due diligence steps:

1) Supplier questionnaire covering security governance, controls, and operational practices.

2) Review of assurance reports (e.g., SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001 certificates) where available.

3) Assessment of data handling practices, encryption, access controls, and incident response/breach notification.

4) Review of subcontracting arrangements and data location/processing locations.

5) Financial and operational stability assessment for critical providers.

6) Assessment of regulatory constraints and required approvals (where applicable).

High-risk engagements may require onsite assessment, penetration testing evidence, or
additional assurance activities.

6. Security and Architecture Assessment

Information Security and ICT architecture must assess:

- Authentication and access control model (MFA, SSO, privileged access controls).

- Network integration and segmentation requirements.

- Data protection (encryption in transit/at rest, key management, data segregation).

- Logging, monitoring, and auditability (availability of logs, APIs for integration).

- Vulnerability management and patching practices.

- Secure SDLC maturity (for software providers).

Identified gaps must be documented with remediation commitments and deadlines prior to
onboarding where feasible.

7. Contractual Requirements (Minimum Clauses)

Contracts for ICT third parties must include (as applicable):

- Confidentiality and data protection obligations aligned to CBG classification.

- Right to audit and/or access to independent assurance reports.

- Incident and breach notification timelines and cooperation obligations.

- Subcontractor controls and approval requirements.

- Service levels, availability commitments, and support response times.

- Business continuity and disaster recovery obligations, including test frequency and RTO/RPO where relevant.

- Data return/deletion requirements at termination and during transitions.

- Exit and transition assistance obligations for critical services.

8. Onboarding and Go-Live Controls

Before go-live:

1) Confirm completion of due diligence and risk acceptance for outstanding issues.

2) Ensure security requirements are implemented (MFA, logging, encryption).

3) Validate integration testing, including failure scenarios and recovery procedures.

4) Register the supplier in the third-party inventory with ownership, classification, and review cadence.

5) Establish operational contacts, escalation paths, and incident communication channels.

9. Ongoing Monitoring

Ongoing monitoring must include:

- Annual (or more frequent for critical providers) reassessment of security posture and assurance reports.

- Review of SLA performance and incidents.

- Monitoring of significant changes (subprocessors, platform changes, data location changes).

- Concentration risk monitoring for critical vendors used across multiple services.

Any material adverse findings must be escalated to ICT Risk Management and relevant
governance committees.

10. Exit Planning and Portability

For critical services, an exit plan is mandatory and must include:

- Data portability mechanisms and formats.

- Technical migration approach (e.g., parallel run, cutover plans).

- Contractual obligations for transition support.

- Backup and contingency measures to reduce lock-in.

Exit plans must be reviewed at least annually and after material changes.

11. Records and Retention

Due diligence artifacts, risk assessments, contracts, and monitoring evidence must be
retained for audit and regulatory review. Records must be linked to the third-party
inventory entry and maintained for the required retention period.
