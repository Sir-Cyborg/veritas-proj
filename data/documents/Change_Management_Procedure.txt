1. Introduction

Uncontrolled changes are a major driver of outages, security incidents, and operational
risk. Change Management ensures that changes to ICT services are assessed, approved,
implemented, and reviewed in a controlled and auditable manner.

This procedure defines how CBG plans, approves, implements, and reviews changes to
applications, infrastructure, configurations, and cloud resources, including standard,
normal, and emergency changes.

2. Scope

This procedure applies to changes affecting:

- Production systems and services.

- Shared platforms (identity, network, databases, monitoring).

- Security controls and configurations (firewall rules, IAM policies).

- Cloud infrastructure and infrastructure-as-code deployments.

Routine operational activities that do not change state (e.g., log review) are not changes.
Patching, configuration adjustments, and deployments are changes and must follow this
procedure.

3. Change Types and Definitions

3.1 Standard Change

A low-risk, pre-approved, repeatable change with a proven procedure and predefined testing
(e.g., routine certificate renewal using a documented runbook).

3.2 Normal Change

A change requiring assessment and approval by Change Advisory Board (CAB) or delegated
approvers based on risk and impact.

3.3 Emergency Change

A change that must be implemented urgently to restore service or address a critical security
risk. Emergency changes require expedited approval and post-implementation review.

4. Roles and Responsibilities

- Change Requestor: prepares the change record with required detail and coordinates implementation.

- Implementer: executes the change steps and validates outcomes.

- Service Owner: accountable for business impact decisions and approves changes for their service.

- CAB: reviews and approves normal changes based on risk/impact; ensures scheduling coordination.

- Change Manager: governs the process, ensures record quality, and chairs CAB meetings.

- Information Security/IAM: reviews changes that affect security controls or sensitive environments.

5. Change Record Requirements

All changes must be recorded in the ITSM tool and include at minimum:

- Scope and affected services/configuration items.

- Business justification and expected benefits.

- Risk assessment (impact, likelihood, rollback complexity).

- Implementation plan with step-by-step tasks.

- Testing evidence and validation plan.

- Rollback/backout plan and decision criteria for aborting.

- Communication plan and stakeholder notifications.

- Scheduling details and maintenance window alignment.

6. Risk Assessment and Approval

Approval levels depend on risk and scope. Factors include:

- Customer impact potential, including digital channel availability and payment processing.

- Data integrity risk (transaction postings, balances).

- Security impact (IAM policies, encryption, firewall rules).

- Complexity and novelty of the change.

Low-risk standard changes may be auto-approved but must follow the documented procedure.
Normal changes require CAB or delegated approval. High-risk changes require additional
approvals, including Information Security review where applicable.

7. Testing and Pre-Implementation Controls

Changes must be tested in lower environments where feasible. Minimum expectations:

- Functional testing for application releases.

- Regression testing for critical services.

- Security testing when security-related components change.

- Performance checks where relevant (capacity-sensitive systems).

Evidence (test results, deployment pipeline logs) must be attached to the change record.

8. Implementation and Validation

Implementation must follow the approved plan. During execution:

1) Confirm prerequisite conditions and approvals.

2) Notify stakeholders according to the communication plan.

3) Execute steps with appropriate segregation of duties.

4) Validate success using technical monitoring and business validation where appropriate.

5) Monitor for stabilization after completion.

Any deviations from the approved plan must be documented in the change record.

9. Emergency Changes

Emergency changes may be initiated for:

- P1 incidents requiring immediate restoration.

- Critical vulnerabilities requiring urgent mitigation.

Emergency change requirements:

- Document rationale and risk assessment (brief but explicit).

- Obtain expedited approval from authorized approver(s) (on-call manager/service owner).

- Execute with enhanced monitoring and maintain a clear rollback plan.

- Conduct a post-implementation review within 5 business days to validate controls and prevent recurrence.

10. Post-Implementation Review and Closure

After implementation, the Change Requestor must update the record with:

- Outcome (successful, partially successful, failed, rolled back).

- Actual start/end times and any issues encountered.

- Validation evidence and monitoring results.

- Lessons learned and follow-up actions if needed.

Failed or high-impact changes must be reviewed by CAB and may trigger Problem Management.

11. Change Calendar and Blackout Windows

Changes must be scheduled considering business critical periods (e.g., month-end close,
regulatory reporting windows). Blackout windows must be defined and enforced for critical
systems. Exceptions require senior approval and enhanced controls.

12. Metrics and Reporting

Change Management metrics include:

- Change success rate and rollback rate.

- Emergency change volume and root causes.

- Change-related incident rate (incidents within 72 hours of a change).

- Lead time and record quality compliance.

Metrics are reviewed monthly by ICT governance.
