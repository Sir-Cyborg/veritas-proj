1. Introduction

Corporate Banking Group (CBG) processes a wide range of information, including customer
data, financial transactions, trading positions, internal strategy documents, and technical
information. Data must be managed and protected according to its sensitivity, business
value, and regulatory requirements.

This policy defines data classification levels and the minimum handling requirements for
each level to protect confidentiality, integrity, and availability. It supports consistent
controls across ICT systems, business processes, and supplier arrangements.

2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- All data created, received, processed, stored, or transmitted by CBG in any format (digital, paper, voice).

- All systems and repositories, including endpoints, file shares, email, collaboration platforms, databases, cloud services, and backups.

- All third parties processing CBG data on CBG's behalf.

It applies to both structured and unstructured data, including logs that may contain
personal or sensitive information.

3. Roles and Responsibilities

- Data Owner: accountable for classification, access rules, and retention decisions for a dataset or information domain.

- Data Custodian (often ICT): implements technical controls in systems storing/processing the data.

- Users: handle data according to classification and report misclassification or mishandling.

- Information Security: defines protective control expectations and monitors compliance.

- Compliance/Legal: interprets regulatory and contractual requirements.

4. Data Classification Levels

CBG uses the following classification levels. When in doubt, classify at the higher level
until confirmed by the Data Owner.

4.1 Public

Information approved for public release (e.g., published reports, marketing materials).

4.2 Internal

Information intended for internal use that could cause minor harm if disclosed (e.g.,
internal procedures, organizational charts).

4.3 Confidential

Sensitive information that could cause significant harm if disclosed, including customer
data, internal financial information, security architecture details, and non-public business
plans.

4.4 Restricted

Highly sensitive information requiring the strongest protections, including: authentication
secrets, cryptographic keys, highly sensitive customer identifiers, material non-public
information (MNPI) where applicable, and core banking transaction integrity data.

5. Classification Rules

Data Owners must classify datasets at creation or onboarding into a system. Systems
processing multiple classifications must apply controls based on the highest classification
present. Derived data must not be classified lower than its source unless formally approved.
Aggregation can increase sensitivity; for example, a combined dataset of transactions and
customer identifiers may be Restricted even if each component dataset is Confidential.

6. Handling Requirements by Classification

6.1 Access Control

Public: no access restrictions beyond integrity controls.

Internal: access limited to CBG users with a business need.

Confidential: role-based access; least privilege; MFA required for remote access; access
reviews at least quarterly for critical datasets.

Restricted: strict role-based access; MFA required for all access; privileged access must
use PAM; enhanced monitoring and logging.

6.2 Storage and Encryption

Confidential and Restricted data must be encrypted in transit using approved protocols.
Encryption at rest is required where feasible, especially for cloud storage, databases, and
backups. Restricted data must not be stored on unmanaged devices or unapproved locations.

6.3 Transmission and Sharing

Confidential and Restricted data sharing must use approved secure channels. Emailing
Restricted data is prohibited unless approved secure encryption mechanisms are used and the
Data Owner approves. Sharing with third parties requires contract controls and due
diligence.

6.4 Printing and Physical Handling

Confidential and Restricted data must be printed only when necessary and must be stored
securely (locked cabinets) and disposed via secure shredding or approved disposal services.

6.5 Logging and Monitoring

Systems handling Confidential and Restricted data must generate audit logs for access and
key changes. Restricted data systems require enhanced monitoring for anomalous access
patterns.

7. Data Retention and Disposal

Retention periods must be defined by Data Owners in collaboration with Compliance/Legal and
must reflect regulatory and business requirements. When retention expires, data must be
disposed securely to prevent reconstruction. Disposal methods must be appropriate to media
type (secure wipe for disks, cryptographic erase where supported, physical destruction where
necessary). Backups must follow the same retention and disposal requirements.

8. Data Loss Prevention and Incident Reporting

CBG uses data loss prevention (DLP) controls where appropriate. Suspected loss, leakage, or
unauthorized disclosure of Confidential or Restricted data must be reported immediately via
the Incident Management process and escalated to Information Security and Compliance as
required.

9. Third-Party Data Handling

Third parties processing Confidential or Restricted data must meet CBG security
requirements, including encryption, access controls, logging, and breach notification.
Contracts must specify data ownership, permitted use, retention, and return or deletion
obligations at contract end.

10. Exceptions and Non-Compliance

Exceptions require Data Owner approval and Information Security review for
Confidential/Restricted data. Non-compliance may result in access restrictions, remediation
plans, disciplinary action, and supplier enforcement actions.
