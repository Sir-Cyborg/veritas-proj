1. Introduction

Information and Communication Technology (ICT) systems are critical assets for Corporate
Banking Group (CBG). These systems enable customer onboarding, payments, lending operations,
treasury services, regulatory reporting, and internal collaboration. The purpose of this ICT
Security Policy is to define high-level principles and mandatory security rules for
protecting information, systems, and services against accidental or malicious loss, misuse,
disruption, or unauthorized disclosure.

This policy establishes minimum security requirements for all ICT environments used to
store, process, or transmit CBG information, including on-premise infrastructure, endpoints,
cloud services, container platforms, and third-party managed systems. Detailed technical
standards and procedures (e.g., hardening baselines, patching procedures, incident response
runbooks) must align to this policy.

2. Purpose and Objectives

The objectives of this policy are to:

- Protect confidentiality, integrity, and availability of CBG information and services.

- Reduce ICT security risk to a level consistent with CBG risk appetite and regulatory obligations.

- Provide a consistent control baseline across business units, subsidiaries, and outsourced arrangements.

- Ensure that security is integrated into governance, operations, and software delivery.

- Define accountability and enforce compliance through monitoring, reporting, and audit.

3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- All employees, contractors, consultants, and temporary staff who access or manage CBG ICT resources.

- All information assets, including data, applications, infrastructure, code repositories, and cryptographic keys.

- All service providers that process CBG data or provide ICT services, including SaaS, IaaS, and managed services.

This policy also applies to pre-production environments whenever they contain production
data, confidential test data, or are connected to production networks.

4. Security Governance Principles

CBG adopts a defense-in-depth security model, combining preventive, detective, and
corrective controls. Security is a shared responsibility across ICT functions, with clear
accountability for control ownership and risk acceptance. The following principles are
mandatory:

- Least privilege: access is limited to what is needed for approved business tasks.

- Secure by default: baseline configurations must be hardened; insecure defaults are prohibited.

- Segregation of duties: critical actions (e.g., deployment approvals, privileged access grants) must be separated.

- Accountability and traceability: actions on systems and data must be logged and attributable to an individual identity.

- Resilience: systems must be designed and operated to withstand failures and recover within defined objectives.

5. Roles and Responsibilities

Key roles include:

- CISO: owns this policy, defines security strategy, and reports material security risks to executive management.

- ICT Risk Management: maintains ICT risk taxonomy, performs risk assessments, and tracks remediation commitments.

- System and Service Owners: accountable for security controls on their services, including remediation of findings.

- Cybersecurity Operations Center (SOC): monitors for threats, performs investigations, and coordinates security incidents.

- Identity and Access Management (IAM): operates access controls, privileged access, and authentication services.

- All Users: must follow acceptable use rules, protect credentials, and report suspected incidents promptly.

6. Mandatory Control Requirements

6.1 Asset Inventory and Classification

All ICT assets must be inventoried and assigned an owner. Information assets must be
classified according to the Data Classification Policy, and systems must inherit security
controls based on the highest classification processed.

6.2 Identity, Authentication, and Access Control

Unique user identities are mandatory. Multi-factor authentication (MFA) is required for
remote access, privileged access, and access to sensitive data. Shared accounts are
prohibited except for approved service accounts with documented ownership, rotation of
secrets, and compensating monitoring.

6.3 Secure Configuration and Hardening

Systems must comply with approved hardening standards (e.g., CIS-based baselines or internal
equivalents). Unnecessary services, default credentials, and unused open ports must be
removed or disabled. Administrative interfaces must not be exposed to the internet unless
explicitly approved with additional protections.

6.4 Vulnerability and Patch Management

Security patches must be applied based on risk-based timelines. Critical vulnerabilities
with active exploitation indicators require accelerated remediation or compensating
controls. Exceptions must be documented, approved, time-bound, and reviewed.

6.5 Cryptography and Key Management

Sensitive data must be protected in transit using strong, modern encryption. Sensitive data
at rest must be encrypted where feasible. Cryptographic keys must be managed using approved
key management services, with access restrictions, rotation, backup, and logging.

6.6 Logging, Monitoring, and Detection

Security-relevant events must be logged and retained according to retention standards. Logs
must be protected from tampering and forwarded to centralized monitoring where feasible.
Alerting must exist for high-risk events such as privileged access, authentication
anomalies, and changes to security configurations.

6.7 Secure Software Delivery

All software delivery pipelines must include security controls, including code reviews,
dependency management, and security testing appropriate to the risk of the application.
High-risk changes require additional approvals and evidence.

6.8 Third-Party Security

Third parties must be assessed prior to onboarding and monitored during the contract.
Contracts must include security requirements such as breach notification timelines, audit
rights, and data handling obligations.

7. Acceptable Use and User Obligations

Users must:

- Use CBG resources for approved business purposes.

- Protect credentials and never share passwords or MFA tokens.

- Lock screens when unattended and use approved devices for sensitive work.

- Store data only in approved repositories and follow data handling rules.

- Report suspected phishing, malware, data loss, or security incidents immediately to the Service Desk or SOC.

8. Risk Acceptance, Exceptions, and Compensating Controls

Deviations from this policy require documented risk acceptance. The request must include
scope, justification, impact analysis, proposed compensating controls, and an expiry date.
Approval levels depend on the classification and business impact, and may require sign-off
by the Service Owner, ICT Risk Management, and the CISO. Exceptions must be tracked and
reviewed at least quarterly.

9. Compliance, Monitoring, and Audit

Compliance with this policy is mandatory. Compliance is monitored via control testing,
vulnerability scanning, configuration compliance checks, log reviews, audit assessments, and
supplier assurance activities. Non-compliance may result in remediation plans, restriction
of access, disciplinary action, and/or supplier contract enforcement. Material deficiencies
must be escalated to executive management and risk committees.
