Identity & Access Management

Document Control

1. Introduction

Access Management ensures that only authorized individuals and services can access CBG
systems and data, and that such access is appropriate to their job functions. Strong access
controls reduce the likelihood of unauthorized transactions, data breaches, and operational
errors.

This procedure defines end-to-end processes for requesting, approving, granting, reviewing,
and revoking access to ICT resources within Corporate Banking Group (CBG), including
privileged access and service accounts.

2. Scope

This procedure applies to:

- User accounts (employees, contractors, third parties) and their access to applications, databases, infrastructure, and cloud services.

- Privileged accounts (administrators, root, break-glass access) and privileged activities.

- Service accounts and machine identities used by applications, integrations, and automation.

- Access to sensitive datasets and regulated systems.

Physical access controls and building badges are managed by Facilities Security and are
outside the scope of this procedure.

3. Definitions

3.1 Identity: a unique representation of a person or service in an authentication system.

3.2 Privileged Access: access that allows administrative control, elevated permissions, or
the ability to bypass standard controls.

3.3 Least Privilege: granting only the minimum access required to perform authorized tasks.

3.4 Segregation of Duties (SoD): splitting critical tasks across roles to prevent fraud or
errors.

4. Roles and Responsibilities

- Requestor: initiates an access request and provides justification and required scope.

- Line Manager: validates business need and approves request for their direct reports or contractors.

- System/Service Owner: approves access to their system and validates role alignment and SoD requirements.

- IAM Team: configures access, ensures policy compliance, and maintains identity lifecycle processes.

- SOC: monitors privileged activity and investigates anomalies.

- ICT Risk Management: reviews exceptions, SoD conflicts, and high-risk access patterns.

5. Identity Lifecycle Management

5.1 Joiner Process

1) HR triggers identity creation upon onboarding approval.

2) IAM provisions a unique user identity, assigns baseline access (email, collaboration tools), and enforces MFA enrollment.

3) Additional access requires a formal request per Section 6.

5.2 Mover Process

1) Role changes must trigger access review and adjustment within 5 business days.

2) Access not required for the new role must be removed promptly.

5.3 Leaver Process

1) HR notifies termination date/time. For immediate terminations, notification must be immediate.

2) IAM disables accounts and remote access, revokes tokens, and removes access to critical systems within the defined timelines.

3) Privileged credentials and shared secrets known to the leaver must be rotated as required.

5.4 Contractor and Third-Party Accounts

All non-employee accounts must have an internal sponsor, an expiry date, and periodic
revalidation.

6. Access Request and Approval Workflow

All non-baseline access must be requested via the approved access request system and
include:

- System name and environment (prod/non-prod).

- Access level/role requested (read, write, admin) and justification.

- Data classification of the information to be accessed.

- Start date and end date (mandatory for temporary access).

Approval rules:

1) Line Manager approval is required for user access.

2) System/Service Owner approval is required for access to their systems.

3) Additional approvals are required for sensitive systems (e.g., payments, customer data) and privileged access.

4) IAM validates SoD conflicts and risk controls before provisioning.

7. Privileged Access Management (PAM)

Privileged access must be controlled through an approved Privileged Access Management
solution where feasible.

Mandatory requirements:

- Separate privileged accounts: privileged access must not be performed using standard user accounts.

- MFA for all privileged access; step-up authentication for high-risk actions.

- Time-bound elevation (just-in-time access) whenever supported.

- Session recording or command logging for administrative sessions on critical systems.

- Break-glass access must be strictly limited, monitored, and reviewed after use.

Privileged access requests must include a task reference (change ticket, incident ticket)
and must be approved by the System Owner.

8. Service Accounts and Machine Identities

Service accounts must be created only when necessary and must have:

- Documented ownership and purpose.

- Minimal permissions scoped to the service function.

- Credentials stored in approved secret management solutions.

- Rotation policy and monitoring for anomalous usage.

Service accounts must not be used for interactive logins except for approved maintenance
scenarios.

9. Access Reviews and Recertification

Access to critical systems and sensitive data must be reviewed at least quarterly. Minimum
review steps:

1) System Owner receives a list of current entitlements and validates each user and access level.

2) Access without clear business justification must be removed.

3) SoD conflicts must be documented and remediated or formally accepted with compensating controls.

4) Review completion evidence must be retained for audit.

10. Revocation and Emergency Removal

Access must be revoked promptly when:

- Employment or contract ends.

- Business need ends (project completed, role changed).

- Policy violations occur (e.g., credential sharing) or compromise is suspected.

For suspected compromise, SOC may request immediate access removal and credential resets.
Emergency removals must be documented and communicated to relevant owners.

11. Records and Retention

Access requests, approvals, provisioning logs, and access reviews must be retained according
to retention policies. Privileged access logs and session recordings must be retained for
the duration required by security monitoring and regulatory needs.
