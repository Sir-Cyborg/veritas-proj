1. Introduction

Operational resilience is essential for Corporate Banking Group (CBG). Backup and Disaster
Recovery (DR) capabilities enable the restoration of data and services after accidental
deletion, corruption, ransomware events, infrastructure failures, or site-level outages.

This procedure defines minimum requirements and steps for backup creation, validation,
retention, recovery execution, and disaster recovery testing for ICT services. It ensures
that recovery objectives are defined, achievable, and evidenced.

2. Scope

This procedure applies to:

- Databases and data stores supporting banking applications.

- File systems, object storage, and collaboration repositories storing business or technical data.

- Virtual machines, containers, and platform services that require backup or configuration recovery.

- Critical cloud resources and infrastructure-as-code state.

It includes both production and critical non-production systems where required for service
restoration.

3. Definitions

3.1 RTO (Recovery Time Objective): the targeted duration of time within which a service must
be restored after a disruption.

3.2 RPO (Recovery Point Objective): the maximum tolerable period in which data might be lost
due to a disruption.

3.3 Backup: a copy of data or system state used to restore after loss/corruption.

3.4 Disaster Recovery: the set of capabilities to restore services after a major disruption
affecting a primary site or critical infrastructure.

4. Roles and Responsibilities

- Service Owner: defines business requirements, including RTO/RPO; ensures DR plans exist and tests are executed.

- Infrastructure Resilience Team: operates backup platforms, validates backups, and coordinates DR exercises.

- Application Teams: ensure application-level backup consistency, participate in recovery tests, and validate business functionality after recovery.

- Information Security: defines ransomware-resilient backup requirements and monitors control effectiveness.

- ICT Risk Management: monitors resilience risks and tracks remediation actions for failed tests or gaps.

5. Backup Policy Requirements (Operational)

5.1 Backup Coverage

All production systems must have a documented backup strategy aligned to RTO/RPO. Systems
processing Confidential or Restricted data must ensure backups are protected to the same
classification requirements.

5.2 Backup Frequency

Backup frequency must be sufficient to meet RPO. For transactional systems, this may require
point-in-time recovery mechanisms, log shipping, or continuous replication.

5.3 Retention

Retention periods must align with regulatory and business requirements. At minimum, backups
must support restoration for operational recovery scenarios and may be longer for regulatory
records where applicable.

5.4 Backup Integrity and Immutability

Backups must be protected from tampering and ransomware. Where feasible, immutable or write-
once storage must be used for critical datasets. Backup credentials must be protected using
least privilege and monitored.

6. Backup Implementation Standards

Minimum standards include:

- Encryption in transit and at rest for Confidential/Restricted backups.

- Separation of backup administration from day-to-day system administration where feasible.

- Centralized monitoring of backup job success/failure and capacity.

- Regular verification of restore capability (see Section 8).

For cloud environments, native backup services may be used if they meet security and
retention requirements. Infrastructure-as-code should be backed up by retaining source
repositories, configuration state, and deployment artifacts.

7. Recovery Execution Procedure

Recovery may be initiated due to data corruption, accidental deletion, ransomware, or
infrastructure failure. Steps:

1) Initiate an incident ticket and classify severity. Engage Information Security if ransomware or malicious activity is suspected.

2) Identify affected system(s), data scope, and required recovery point/time.

3) Confirm availability of backups, restore points, and required keys/credentials.

4) Select recovery method: file-level restore, database point-in-time restore, full system restore, or failover to replica/DR environment.

5) Execute recovery in a controlled manner, ensuring evidence is captured (job IDs, logs).

6) Validate technical recovery (system health, error rates) and perform application/business validation (transaction integrity checks).

7) Communicate restoration status and document timeline and outcomes in the incident record.

8. Backup and Recovery Testing

8.1 Routine Restore Tests

Service Owners must ensure periodic restore tests are performed. Minimum frequency:

- Critical services: quarterly restore tests.

- Non-critical services: at least annually.

Restore tests must validate that backups are usable and that RPO/RTO targets are feasible.

8.2 Disaster Recovery Exercises

DR exercises must be conducted at least annually for critical services and must include
failover/failback where feasible. Exercises must test:

- Technical failover processes and runbooks.

- Access and authentication in DR environments.

- Data integrity and application functionality validation steps.

- Communications and decision-making processes.

Failed tests or unmet objectives must result in remediation actions tracked to closure.

9. Ransomware Resilience Considerations

For ransomware scenarios, additional controls may be required:

- Offline or immutable backups for critical datasets.

- Segregated backup networks and restricted administrative access.

- Monitoring for mass deletion, encryption patterns, or abnormal backup job behavior.

- Recovery playbooks that include containment steps and evidence preservation.

10. Documentation and Records

Each service must maintain:

- A backup strategy document (frequency, retention, storage location, encryption, owners).

- Recovery runbooks with step-by-step restore procedures.

- Records of backup job monitoring and failed job remediation.

- Evidence of restore tests and DR exercises, including outcomes and lessons learned.

Documentation must be stored in approved repositories and made available for audits.

11. Exceptions

Exceptions to backup frequency, retention, or testing requirements require documented risk
acceptance and approval by the Service Owner and ICT Risk Management. For critical services,
CRO approval may be required depending on impact.
